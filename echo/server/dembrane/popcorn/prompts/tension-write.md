# Tension write

Version: `tension-write-v1.2`

One verified tension from a session, with its two poles and the quotes that
show each being held, is to be written for a screen at the front of a room.
Write its knot and its question. Return the poles as given, except that a
pole longer than seven words is cut to seven, in the holders' own words and
without changing which side it is: the screen reads them in one glance.

## Facets

The input may list facets of the same pull found in other rooms: a middle
course somebody proposed, a reason behind a pole, a consequence of one. A
middle the room reached for belongs in the knot as what it costs ("record
only the synthesis and the breakouts stay uncaptured"), not as a resolution,
and never as a third pole. A tension written as all-or-nothing when a room
found a middle has been reopened, and the room will say so.

## The knot

One sentence, eighteen words at most, that says what each side pays if the
other wins, in the room's terms: "Record it and people stop thinking out
loud; don't, and the good bits are gone by Friday." Do not resolve it, do not
recommend, do not soften it into an agreement that was not reached.

A paragraph is not a knot, and a line built from abstract nouns nobody said
(capture, nuance, accountability, engagement, alignment) has failed before it
was read. Write the substance, never the meeting: strip the fact that a
conversation happened and the sentence must still say something true about
the world. Nothing beginning with people discussing, recognising,
acknowledging, expressing or feeling.

## The question

One sentence, twenty-two words at most, naming the question the group has to
answer for this to move, as the open question it is. Test it: could it be
answered in a way that costs one pole something real? If every answer leaves
both poles intact ("how can we do X without losing Y"), or it is a
recommendation with a question mark ("should we do Z"), it has resolved the
tension on the page instead of in the room. The question has to leave the
room a real choice, including the choice to give something up.

Both lines are checked mechanically after the run and sent back if they break
the limits.

Return only the structured output requested by the caller.
