# Millbrook Citizens' Assembly on Home Heating: Final Report and Recommendations

Over the weekend of 2 and 3 July 2026, forty residents of Millbrook gathered at Weavers' Hall, in online rooms, and across outreach sessions at Larkspur Lodge and Market Square to deliberate the future of the town's heating. With the retirement of the local gas network confirmed for 2035, the assembly was tasked not with rubber-stamping pre-cooked municipal proposals, but with establishing the ground rules, non-negotiable protections, and trade-offs acceptable to the community. Across twenty-five distinct discussions, participants made clear that while the end of mains gas is understood as an engineering inevitability, public support for alternatives: individual heat pumps, a shared River Mell heat network, deep insulation, or direct electric systems: is entirely provisional. Residents refused to accept market-driven models that treat households as isolated consumers, demanding instead that Millbrook Town Council assume collective civic responsibility for financing, technical risk, grid resilience, and tenancy rights. What follows is the assembly's direct advice, structured around ten critical questions that local decision-makers must answer before cutting a single gas main.

### How should the town sequence neighbourhood transitions without creating first-mover penalties?

Sequencing emerged as an immediate flashpoint between collective efficiency and individual autonomy. In Table 1, retired postman Gareth from Quarry Row insisted that first-generation equipment is always "dear as poison and full of bugs", warning that forcing early adoption would cause "total rebellion on the doorstep." District nurse Priya from Hopfield Road supported this caution, noting that an air-source heat pump quote of 14,800 euros left a 9,000-euro capital shortfall that single-earner households cannot absorb. Retired bookkeeper Beatrix calculated that pepper-potting installations house-by-house down Victorian streets wastes thousands of euros on piecemeal scaffolding and repetitive pavement trenching, urging a whole-street approach. When Day 2 opened in the plenary room, Ray from Hopfield Road demanded an immediate vote on individual grants, but Fiona from Osier Street fiercely objected that a premature vote would be "turkeys voting for an early christmas", permanently trapping uninsulated terraces behind wealthier modern properties.

The assembly concluded that street-level coordination is essential, but rejected compulsory mandates. Table 1, supported by the Day 1 plenary debrief, agreed that whole-street conversions should proceed only if an 80 percent collective street threshold is reached through neighbour buddy schemes and live demonstration homes, rather than official council circulars. Furthermore, early adoption is supported only if capital costs are underwritten up front by public funding so working families face zero upfront debt, and only if the town provides a guaranteed forty-eight-hour emergency callout service staffed by local technicians trained at Millbrook College.

### What conditions must Millbrook Town Council meet before building a River Mell district heat network?

Drawing water from the River Mell at the disused paper mill site to supply a town-wide district loop elicited strong technical enthusiasm, but acute wariness regarding cost and governance. In Table 3, former paper mill process engineer Rafael demonstrated that the river provides an exceptional thermal resource, maintaining flow rates of three to four cubic metres a second and water temperatures rarely below five degrees Celsius in winter. However, heating engineer Bartek countered that connecting five hundred homes creates a captive consumer base with no ability to switch suppliers if tariffs surge, warning that private district schemes routinely trap residents behind steep monthly standing charges. In the community buildings table, Mell Valley leisure centre manager Idris noted his pool consumes 94,000 euros of gas annually, offering an ideal 35 percent baseline anchor load. Yet Marjorie from Weavers' Hall warned that her charity's 3,800-euro reserve fund would be wiped out instantly by commercial connection charges.

The assembly established four mandatory conditions for the River Mell network. Table 3, Table 11, and the community buildings table stipulated that the network may proceed only if it remains under 100 percent municipal or community-benefit ownership, legally prohibiting the sale of infrastructure concessions to private equity. Second, the heat tariff must be subject to an iron-clad statutory cap pegged below retail gas equivalents (specified in Table 3 at ten to eleven cents per kilowatt-hour), coupled with an explicit, penalty-free legal right to disconnect. Third, public anchor loads: the leisure centre, Larkspur Lodge care home, and Millbrook Primary School: must be connected first to de-risk the central plant before residential streets are disrupted. Finally, head teacher Chinyere Okafor secured agreement that during extreme winter freezes, an enforceable curtailment protocol must prioritise warm classrooms and frail residents over public swimming pool water.

### How can public policy protect private tenants from renoviction and unaffordable bills?

The split incentive between landlords and private tenants generated the fiercest friction of the assembly. In Table 2, Callum, who lets four Victorian terraces on Osier Street, stated plainly that spending 14,000 euros per house on heat pumps represents a "pure penalty" with zero rental return, warning that rent controls would force landlords to sell to outside corporate funds, delay until 2035, or fit cheap electric panel heaters. Tamsin, renting a mould-infested flat on Quarry Row, confronted him directly, stating that her daughter's chronic bronchitis represents the landlord's "unpaid sixty thousand euros" and arguing that public subsidies without conditions amount to corporate welfare. These concerns were reinforced in Table 12, where staff nurse Zara and barista Theo described enduring freezing bedrooms and spending 80 euros a week on plug-in heaters, trapped by the constant fear that reporting damp or poor energy efficiency would trigger retaliatory no-fault evictions.

The assembly resolved that public decarbonisation funds must never finance tenant displacement. Table 2, Table 4, and Table 12 agreed that council retrofit grants should be issued to private landlords only if accompanied by a legally binding five-year rent freeze and an iron-clad ban on no-fault evictions. To eliminate the loophole of landlords installing ruinously expensive direct electric resistance heaters, Table 12 demanded proactive street-by-street council inspections funded by an annual 300-euro landlord licensing fee. Finally, doctoral researcher Niamh and Theo recommended introducing a portable warm-home credit that travels directly with verified low-income tenants, ensuring public financial support warms the person rather than subsidising the landlord's capital asset.

### What specific protections are required for multi-occupancy blocks and leaseholders?

Flatted blocks presented distinct legal and operational deadlocks. In Table 6, Heron Tower residents on the Kestrel Estate detailed the agony of living under an obsolete 1998 communal gas boiler. Ninth-floor tenant Sadia described flats reaching 32 degrees in October because the system operates on inflexible calendar dates, calling it "heating the sky", while depot night-worker Leon on the third floor lost sleep to shuddering risers and paid twice by running electric fan heaters. Marguerite, a Right to Buy leaseholder, feared a 24,000-euro major works assessment that would force her to sell her home. Similar legal paralysis dominated Table 9, where Canal Wharf leaseholders Esther, Vikram, and Poppy explained that their managing agent doubled service charges to 3,100 euros, while freeholder rules prohibited external heat pump units or double-glazed Crittall replacements.

The conditions drafted across Table 6, Table 9, and the Day 2 plenary insist that multi-occupancy housing receive tailored statutory frameworks. The council must secure whole-building decarbonisation funding that protects leaseholders by capping capital contributions at 5,000 euros (with total exemptions for low-income pensioners), while legally preventing social housing landlords from recouping capital shortfalls through permanent core rent increases. For Heron Tower, tenant support requires replacing calendar heating with individual digital heat interface units that guarantee an ambient baseline of 16 to 17 degrees to prevent inter-flat condensation, alongside mandatory acoustic dampening in risers. At Canal Wharf, leaseholders must receive a statutory 51 percent majority veto over contractor selection and specification, ensuring distant managing agents cannot levy inflated administration fees on public retrofit works.

### Can the local electrical grid cope with mass heat pump adoption, and who pays for upgrades?

The physical capacity of Millbrook's electrical distribution network provoked deep scepticism from practical trades and shift workers. In Online Room 3, software tester Eun-ji noted that neighbourhood transformers already hum loudly when car chargers activate on Hopfield Road, while electrical contractor Martin warned the Day 2 plenary that Quarry Row relies on seventy-year-old looped supply cables requiring at least 3 million euros in low-voltage reinforcement before widespread heat pump uptake is viable. Fergus, a warehouse commuter living on Osier Street, attacked mandatory time-of-use tariffs as an extortionate "penalty tax for having an out of town job", pointing out that working commuters cannot trickle-heat uninsulated solid brick terraces in the middle of the night. In the trades table, electrical contractor Sunita reported that hundreds of homes still rely on 60-amp main fuses and rewirable boards, requiring up to 2,000 euros in electrical remedial works before heating plant can be touched.

The assembly concluded that electrification must not outpace physical infrastructure. Online Room 3 and the trades table stipulated that mass heat pump deployment can proceed only if the distribution network operator conducts and publicly publishes street-level cable capacity audits. Grid reinforcement, substation upgrades, and cable unlooping must be funded centrally as public infrastructure, explicitly prohibiting individual homeowners or businesses from being billed direct connection surcharges. Furthermore, Online Room 3 demanded a legally enforceable ceiling on peak domestic electricity rates, backed by mandatory physical boost buttons on home controllers and automatic fallbacks to baseline tariffs whenever automated load-management software fails.

### How must the council adapt its approach for historic stone cottages and conservation zones?

Strict heritage planning rules in St Aldric's Yard, Market Square, and Tanner's Walk directly conflict with standard retrofit assumptions. In the heritage cottages table, Perpetua, Laszlo, and Hamish explained that applying conventional impermeable insulation or external cladding to 1785 river-stone cottages traps interstitial condensation, causing structural timber joists to rot within seasons. Hamish recounted paying 750 euros for architectural drawings merely to apply for internal secondary glazing, only to be refused. A sharp debate emerged between Laszlo, who prioritised the preservation of historic joinery and hand-hewn oak roof structures, and Hamish, who condemned rigid conservation policies for keeping residents "frozen in amber like a museum exhibit" whilst energy bills reach 340 euros a month.

The heritage table, reinforced by Eileen Roberts' testimony at the Saturday market pop-up, established that stone properties can decarbonise only under bespoke conservation rules. Residents agreed to switch away from gas combis only if the town council publishes a binding pattern book of deemed consents, granting automatic approval for approved breathable wood-fibre systems, slimline magnetic secondary glazing, and internal heat interface units. For St Aldric's Yard, the group backed connection to the River Mell heat network only if flow temperatures are supplied at 70 degrees Celsius to preserve existing cast-iron radiators, and only if street excavations are relaid with original pennant stone flags by specialist conservation masons rather than patched with tarmac. Crucially, Hamish insisted that the town council fund the price premium between standard modern drywall and heritage-grade breathable lime materials, arguing that because aesthetic preservation serves the wider public, the public purse must shoulder the cost.

### Why are off-grid rural settlements resisting town-wide electrification plans?

Residents of Thornby Hollow felt systematically sidelined by an assembly design that assumed every household was hooked to an urban gas pipe. In Table 10 and the plenary sessions, sheep farmer Aled, holiday-let host Greta, and retired teacher Rufus reminded the assembly that their hamlet relies entirely on kerosene oil, wood stoves, and bottled gas. Aled explained that winter freezes in the hollow leave solar panels useless, while ground-source quotes of 22,000 euros would devour his entire annual farming income. Greta pointed out that the regional grid operator quoted 14,000 euros simply to reinforce her property's electric supply. Rufus vigorously defended his seasoned ash logs, noting that when winter gales sever the single-phase overhead line from the valley road: leaving the hamlet blacked out for up to forty hours: timber stoves are an indispensable survival mechanism.

Table 10 and rural speaker Marian formulated an uncompromising rural red line. Thornby Hollow and outlying properties will participate in decarbonisation mandates only if the distribution network operator replaces fragile wooden overhead poles with heavy-duty underground three-phase cables at zero direct capital cost to rural households. Additionally, the town council must provide explicit statutory guarantees that off-grid rural properties retain the permanent legal right to burn local wood in secondary stoves during emergency power outages, rejecting any town-wide smoke control bans that treat isolated farmsteads like high street shopping precincts.

### How do we guarantee physical safety, health, and dignity for medically vulnerable residents?

For older and disabled residents, heat is an absolute clinical requirement rather than a flexible consumer choice. During the specialised outreach session at Larkspur Lodge care home, registered manager Bernadette and senior care assistant Joseph explained that frail residents experience temperatures below 22 degrees as physically dangerous, with Joseph describing how core body temperatures plummet on the night shift. Residents Ivy, ninety-one, and Clarence, eighty-eight, rejected heat pumps that produce lukewarm radiator surfaces, arguing that thin circulation requires direct radiant warmth and that ceiling fan-coil units cause distressing draughts and cognitive agitation. In Table 5, eighty-four-year-old Winifred from Linden Terrace recounted being terrorised by an aggressive doorstep spray-foam salesman, while in Online Room 2, Morwenna described how a power outage threatens the life of her son Callum, who relies on an electric ventilator and profiling bed on Hopfield Road. Full-time power-wheelchair user Isaac added that contractors routinely trap disabled residents inside their homes by dumping trench spoils across dropped kerbs.

The assembly made support for vulnerable households contingent on rigorous physical safeguards. Table 5, Online Room 2, and the care home visit dictated that the council establish a legally binding priority register before any gas decommissioning occurs. Every medically dependent household must be provided with fully funded home battery storage capable of sustaining life-support and heating loads for at least twelve hours, backed by a municipal mobile generator fleet with a mandatory ninety-minute deployment guarantee. Doorstep energy sales and unsolicited cold calling must be banned entirely under local bylaws, replaced by a walk-in, face-to-face civic advice shop in Market Square. For residential care facilities like Larkspur Lodge, Bernadette secured the condition that connection to district heating must include on-site automated secondary backup heating and a ring-fenced social care energy tariff that caps running costs at gas parity, preventing switchover costs from inflating resident fees. Finally, Isaac secured the requirement that contractors blocking dropped kerbs face mandatory 500-euro daily municipal fines.

### How can Millbrook secure local trade capacity and prevent poor-quality contractor installations?

The assembly repeatedly warned that top-down policies will collapse if local trades are excluded or if national voucher schemes invite predatory contractors into the town. In the trades table, veteran gas engineer Duncan voiced anguish at being treated like a polluting dinosaur after twenty-five years crawling under floors on Christmas Eve, noting he carries 12,000 euros of combi boiler spares that cannot be used on heat pumps. Craig, head of building services at Millbrook College, explained that heat pump training classrooms sit empty because independent sole traders earning 70 euros an hour cannot sacrifice a week's billings to attend unpaid courses. The catastrophic consequences of poor installation were laid bare in Online Room 5, where Orla described an eighteen-month ordeal on Quarry Row following a hasty twenty-minute survey: undersized radiators, an immersion heater that drove January power bills to 340 euros, and a contractor whose phone disconnected permanently. This contrasted with Harriet on Hopfield Road, whose successful heat pump followed a comprehensive four-hour room-by-room calculation and multiple return visits to balance flow temperatures.

The assembly established a strict quality and procurement framework across the trades table, Table 4, Table 8, and Online Room 5. Council retrofit programmes must ring-fence contracts exclusively for accredited local firms, banning distant tier-one consortiums that extract margins while sidelining town trades. To build trade capacity, the council must fund paid training fellowships providing 250 euros a day in direct wage compensation for local heating engineers attending Millbrook College. To protect householders, general builder Declan and mortgage adviser Solveig mandated that public grants follow a phased 30-40-30 payment structure, with the final 30 percent withheld until twenty-eight days post-commissioning, subject to an independent thermal imaging inspection proving the property achieves design temperatures. In Online Room 5, Sol and Orla added that installers must be legally contracted to return three times across the first heating season to fine-tune weather compensation curves, backed by a municipal retention fund held in escrow.

### What communication and commercial measures are necessary to build community trust?

Communication failures and commercial blind spots severely undermine civic confidence in the 2035 target. In Online Room 4, care worker Agnieszka, takeaway operator Mehmet, and grandfather Dinh (assisted by his granddaughter Mai) revealed that council literature routinely triggers panic. Dinh mistook an untranslated English booklet containing words like "decarbonisation" and "retrofit" for an eviction notice on his Linden Terrace bungalow. Mehmet explained that independent traders distrust town hall bureaucracy due to past punitive fines, stressing that high-heat catering operations cannot boil forty litres of broth using low-temperature domestic heat pumps. In Table 7, Market Square café owner Rosalind and butcher Stan emphasized that closing independent shops for even five days of pipe trenching would cause fatal losses of over 4,000 euros. Stan pointed out the irony that his three walk-in refrigeration compressors dump immense volumes of high-grade waste heat into a back alleyway every day, completely ignored by council planners.

The assembly agreed that civic trust requires radical changes in council communication and commercial planning. Online Room 4, Table 7, and Table 11 demanded an immediate end to technical jargon, requiring all future materials to be delivered in plain language and fully translated into community languages, including Polish, Turkish, and Vietnamese. Outreach must bypass council websites and operate through trusted physical community hubs: Weavers' Hall, the Osier Street mosque, and the Quarry Row chapel: provided the council supplies free childcare and avoids political interference in faith governance. For high street businesses, Table 7 and market fishmonger Dan stipulated that street works must be scheduled strictly outside normal trading hours or overnight, backed by daily contractor penalty clauses, 100 percent business rate relief for the duration of works, and a municipal audit to capture commercial refrigeration waste heat before digging new river mains.

***

Millbrook's path to 2035 cannot be built on market optimism, administrative shortcuts, or unbacked mandates. The citizens' assembly has delivered a clear verdict: the town will embrace this transition, but only if the collective purse carries the capital risk, only if the vulnerable are shielded by enforceable clinical and financial guarantees, and only if every neighbourhood moves forward with the certainty that warmth remains a shared public right.
